GSE Makery

↓

The GSE Makery is Stanford's interdisciplinary design studio — a place where researchers, educators, and creators work with their hands. As a Makery Mentor specializing in textiles, I help people develop the technical fluency to make what they're imagining.

My work spans one-on-one guidance on sewing machines, sergers, and embroidery machines, to workshops on garment construction, textile techniques, and repair. The through-line is always the same: helping people understand material in a way that changes how they see it.

Textile Expertise

Teaching textile skills isn't just about technique — it's about giving people a new way to understand what clothing actually is.

When you learn to construct a seam, you start to read every garment differently. You notice the decisions — about material, about structure, about what the maker was trying to say. That shift in perception is what I'm always working toward.

I provide guidance across the full range of textile equipment: sewing machines, sergers, and embroidery machines. From first-time sewers to experienced makers working on research projects, the work is about meeting people where they are and moving them forward.

Workshops

Teaching craft
as a way of seeing.

I lead workshops on garment construction, textile techniques, and creative prototyping. Each one is built around a simple premise: that making something teaches you things about it that looking never can.

One workshop I've developed focuses on clothing repair — fixing what's broken, reinforcing what's wearing thin, extending the life of things worth keeping. In a culture that treats clothes as disposable, knowing how to repair is a form of resistance. And a form of care.

Clothing is never just fabric. It's a series of decisions — about material, about form, about what you want to say. That's the thing I'm always trying to pass on.
